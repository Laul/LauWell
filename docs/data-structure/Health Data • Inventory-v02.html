Health Data Category Map

Every distinct health data point found across the eleven apps in the teardown, each assigned to a category. The vocabulary wasn't designed up front — it was grown one app at a time, mapping each data point onto an existing category or opening a new one.

The vocabulary that emerged

Thirty-seven categories, which is far too many for a single select. They roll up into eight groups — so the practical shape is a two-level scheme: group as the coarse filter, category as the precise one.

The map

One row per distinct data point. First seen in records which app introduced it during the walk, so you can read the order the vocabulary grew in. A highlighted app chip means only that app carries the data point.

What the shape tells you. Sort by Apps carrying it and the distribution is lopsided: a small core of measurement and medication data appears in almost every app, while whole categories — appliance care, supplies, products — exist in exactly one. Those single-app categories are the ones your build has to carry itself, because no platform will hand them to you.
Two judgement calls. Rows are deduplicated — a data point found in six apps is one row listing six apps, not six rows, so the category assignment stays single-valued. And derived scores (sleep score, energy score, health age) were put in Insights & patterns rather than their subject domain, on the grounds that a computed number has different storage, provenance and trust properties than a measured one. Both are reversible if you disagree.
