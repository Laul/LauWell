Patient Data Categories

Seven categories a patient can hold in their head, each with a one-sentence test that settles where a new data point goes, and a tag for the screens that show it. The rows mirror the Notion table Data Model [Simplified]; the earlier five-category map (v03) is kept beside this page.

The dividing axis

Is this value read against a clinical normal range, or against a goal you set yourself? A blood pressure of 160/100 is abnormal to any clinician; nobody reads 4,000 steps as pathological. Clinical band → Vitals. Personal goal → Lifestyle.

One question decides both, and it handles mood without an exception: a daily log with no clinical band, so Lifestyle. Two categories now sit beside the axis rather than on it: Labs, split by where the number comes from, and Ostomy, split by subject so that everything about the stoma is in one place.

The seven, in detail

Sub-category lists are generated from the data map further down, which is the authoritative row set.

Vitals clinical measurement

A measured value with a unit and a timestamp, read against a clinical normal range.

The strongest category in the set, because it describes a shape plus one interpretive test rather than a topic. Anything shaped like value + unit + timestamp, whose number a clinician would call normal or abnormal, is a Vital — so a sensor you connect in 2028 lands here with no new category and no new screen. Shown in Tracker.

Sub-categories
Lab results moved out
By the rule a lab result is a Vital, and it keeps the same storage shape. It now has its own category because it arrives differently — dozens of analytes at once, every few months, on a report — and lives on a different screen (Records). A home glucose reading is Vitals; a fasting glucose from the lab is Labs.
The menstrual cycle sits here, as an exception
Filed under Woman Health so that cycle data has one home next to the body's other signals. Strictly, the axis would put it in Lifestyle (a daily log with no clinical band); the simplified model chose the grouping over the rule.

Labs lab result

A value from a laboratory report, read against the reference range printed on that report.

One row per analyte, grouped by panel — hematology, lipid profile, iron studies — the way a report lays them out. The seed list is docs/data-structure/health_data_labs/common_lab_tests.csv. Storage is the ordinary Measurement with source = lab, plus the reference range the lab printed, because ranges differ between labs and methods. Shown in Records.

Sub-categories (panels)
Keep the lab's range, not a generic one
A result is only "high" relative to the range on its own report. Store the printed range with each result; a generic range is a fallback for display, never the reference.

Medical Records standing fact + care record

A fact with a start date rather than a timestamp, a document, or the record of an encounter with the health system.

Not called "Medical history", because that signals the past — and the active diagnosis list is one of the two things anyone needs in a consultation. Documents, pictures, the medical history and the record of each visit live here. Shown in Records.

Sub-categories
An appointment and an encounter are two records
The appointment is the plan (date, place, who, questions to ask) and lives in Treatments & Appliances, shown in Care. The encounter — Note (Encounter) — is what happened, and lives here. An encounter points to the appointment it is based on, when there is one: an emergency visit has no appointment, and a cancelled appointment never becomes an encounter.
Status, not deletion
A resolved diagnosis stays visible as resolved rather than disappearing. The fact that something ended is itself clinical information.

Lifestyle daily log

Something I logged about my day, measured against a goal I set rather than a clinical range.

This is where the modifiable variables live — the levers you can actually pull — which makes it the input side of nearly every correlation the app will run. Shown in Tracker.

Sub-categories
Cut down on purpose
Nutrition, hydration, caffeine and alcohol, mindfulness, travel and weather were dropped from the simplified model as far from current priorities. They are in the v03 map, ready to come back as rows when needed.

Symptoms self-report

Something I feel and rate myself — no instrument involved.

The separation from Vitals is who produced the value: a device or a lab, versus you. A pain score is a number with a timestamp exactly like a blood pressure, but you generated it, so it's a Symptom. Sub-categories follow body systems, with one general bucket for records not tied to a system. Shown in Tracker.

Sub-categories
The taxonomy problem
Clinical symptom vocabularies (SNOMED CT, ICD-11) are complete, rigorously classified, and hostile to browse. The answer is two vocabularies: a coded reference terminology underneath for correctness and any export to a clinician, and a patient-facing layer on top — everyday names, synonyms so "tummy ache" finds the right code, a body map, and a personal shortlist of the symptoms you actually track. The sub-categories here are that patient-facing layer; they are not the terminology.
Two shapes live here
A rating logged now is a measurement; a headache or a palpitation is an event with an onset and a duration. Both read as Symptoms to the patient but they're different records.

Treatments & Appliances intervention + catalogue

What I'm taking or using, the product behind it, and the care I have planned.

Holds the things — drugs, devices, supplies — and the intervention records that govern them, plus appointments, the forward-looking plan of care. Shown in Care.

Sub-categories
Unfinished, by choice
Only the Appointment row is in the simplified model so far. Medication (catalogue, scheduled / taken / skipped doses, as-needed, supplements, side effects), therapies, devices, supplies and trial periods stay as defined in the v03 map and come back as rows when their screens are built.
Product and act must stay separate entities
The single most consequential decision in the set. Product attributes — convexity, flange size, barrier chemistry, brand, reference — belong to a catalogue entity; the appliance-change event points at it. Only then can you ask "did the 25mm convex barrier give me longer wear time and better skin than the flat one". If product is a free-text field on the event, that question is permanently out of reach.

Ostomy one subject, two rhythms

Anything about the stoma: its output, the skin around it, and the appliance on it.

Ostomy data used to be spread over Vitals, Lifestyle, Symptoms and Treatments. Gathered into one category, one switch hides all of it for someone without a stoma, and a stoma-nurse export reads a single category. Inside it, the split between signals and interventions survives as two sub-categories.

Sub-categories
Daily routines are signals, replacement is an intervention
Output, emptying and skin are read day to day and show in Tracker; the appliance change and the wear time it produces show in Care. Peristomal skin is shown in both, stored once.
A leak is a reason, not an event
A leak forces an appliance change, so it is recorded as that change's reason. Wear time is derived from consecutive changes and is the headline outcome for comparing products.

Categories are not entities

Seven categories sit on top of seven storage shapes, and the mapping is many-to-many. Counts are generated from the map above, so the two cannot drift apart.

Where the model stands

Two changes settled in this revision, three things still open.

01

Labs and Ostomy get their own homes

Labs left Vitals because results arrive as reports and are read in Records; Ostomy gathered rows from four categories so that one switch and one export cover the stoma. Both keep the storage shapes they had.

Resolved
02

Category is not screen

Every row now says which tabs show it. A row is stored once and may appear on several tabs — peristomal skin in Tracker and Care — keeping its category colour on both. Adding a data point to a screen is a tag, not a new destination.

Resolved
03

The catalogue rows are missing

Wherever Catalogue and Event share a sub-category, that's a type table plus an occurrence table: the ostomy product and its changes, the medication and its doses. The simplified model has the events but none of the catalogues yet — the shape matrix shows an empty Catalogue row. They come back with Treatments & Appliances.

Core modelling decision, still to re-add
04

Sub-category names leak across categories

In Notion the sub-category list is one flat select shared by every category, so lab panel names reach other categories: home blood glucose is filed as Vitals → "Biochemistry - Metabolic". "Cardiovascular" exists under Vitals, Labs and Symptoms, which is fine — identity is category plus name — but the Vitals glucose row wants a Vitals name.

Open in Notion
05

Nothing here is an output

Every category describes data going in. Trends, correlations, adherence rates, the History timeline and the stoma-nurse export are a layer over the categories — and only two rows carry the Derived shape. The value sits in joins (Ostomy against Symptoms, Lifestyle against Vitals), which need to be first-class rather than inferred at query time.

Design the joins deliberately

Still outside the seven

Four things that deliberately aren't categories, and what they are instead.

01

Attachments — any record can carry one

Lab PDFs, imaging, letters, product specs, stoma photos. Records → Documents and Pictures hold the ones filed on their own, but a file can also hang off any record in any category, which is why Document is a shape as well. One "all files" view, and unattached files allowed.

02

Emergency view — derived, not stored

Blood type, active conditions, current medications, allergies, contacts. Every element lives in a category already; what's missing is the assembly. It must be reachable without unlocking the app — any design where finding a blood type takes two taps has failed at the only moment it mattered.

03

Provenance and links — fields on every record

Which device, which lab, entered by hand or synced, edited since — and which other records it belongs with (a reading taken during an encounter, a photo about a skin rating). When two sources disagree you need a rule, and when you show a clinician a number they will ask where it came from.

04

Free text — attaches anywhere

The note that doesn't fit a field: what the nurse said, why today was different, a question to remember. A note should attach to any record and also stand alone with a date. Every structured system needs its escape hatch, or people stop using the structured parts.

Where this leaves things

categories, sub-categories, data points, seven storage shapes, and a screen tag on every row. Nothing in the set requires a patient to learn a vocabulary, and nothing in the shape column requires a developer to guess at a table.

The thing to protect from here is the Catalogue plus Event pattern. It looks like an implementation detail and it is actually the feature — the difference between an app that records that you changed a pouch and one that can tell you which pouch works better for you.

Category and sub-category are facets on the data type, not separate tables, so if an assignment turns out wrong in six months it's a config row rather than a migration.
